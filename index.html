The procedure

How it works

The vasectomy procedure is safe and takes approximately 15 minutes. Here is exactly what happens, step by step — no surprises.

  1. A local anaesthetic is injected in the testicle area to manage pain during the procedure. The fine needle feels similar to a flu jab, with slight discomfort.
  2. A tiny incision is made in the scrotum.
  3. The vas is cut, and the tube is left open at the testicular end to help prevent congestion in the testis — reducing the risk of pain and post-vasectomy syndrome.
  4. A layer of tissue is placed at the ends of the vas deferens to prevent them from rejoining.
  5. The procedure is repeated through the same incision for the other testis.
  6. Once complete, the scrotum skin is clipped to heal without stitches. A dressing is applied, and care instructions are provided.

What is a vasectomy?

A vasectomy is a procedure to prevent sperm from entering the ejaculate. The vas deferens — the tube that carries sperm from the testis — is cut, causing sterilisation by stopping sperm flow. Most men won't notice a change in ejaculate volume, as sperm makes up less than 5% of ejaculate.

How long does the procedure take?

A vasectomy takes between 15–20 minutes, depending on the procedure.

What are the types of vasectomy?

Traditional or no-scalpel. The traditional approach uses a scalpel to make an incision on each side of the scrotum. The no-scalpel technique uses one small access point via blunt dissection to reach the vas from both sides — with less chance of bruising and bleeding, and a quicker recovery.

Open-ended or close-ended. Open-ended vasectomy leaves the testicular end of the vas open to allow sperm release into the scrotum, reducing congestion and pressure. Close-ended vasectomy clamps the testicular end with a clip or suture. We perform no-scalpel, open-ended vasectomies.

Can I drive home after my vasectomy?

Yes, it is safe to drive home after a vasectomy.

Can I have it done under sedation or general anaesthetic?

We only offer local anaesthetic for our vasectomy procedure. If you'd prefer sedation or a general anaesthetic, a urologist can provide this with a referral.

Do I need to prepare before my vasectomy?

Yes — you'll find everything you need on our Preparing for your vasectomy page. Please read it before your appointment.

Can I get my vasectomy reversed?

Yes, a vasectomy can be reversed; however the process is costly and not covered by Medicare. If you're asking this question, you may need more time before deciding on permanent contraception via vasectomy.

What are the risks of having a vasectomy?

All surgical procedures carry some risk, and we do all we can to reduce the rate of complications. Common, short-lived symptoms include bruising that fades after a week or so, and mild pain and swelling that settles within a few days.

Less common complications include scrotal haematoma (a large bruise within the scrotum — the chances are significantly reduced by avoiding heavy lifting in the days after your vasectomy), infection (rare, usually mild, and treatable with oral antibiotics), and post-vasectomy pain syndrome (PVPS), a rare complication that can arise at any time post-vasectomy. Pain often reduces naturally, but in rare cases a specialist may need to perform additional surgery. All risks are outlined in your consent form.

How and when do I know the procedure worked?

A vasectomy does not mean immediate sterilisation. You must consider yourself fertile until a semen analysis — performed approximately three months post-vasectomy — confirms the vasectomy was a success and your ‘pipes are clean’.

When can I go back to work?

If your job doesn't involve heavy lifting, you can return immediately. If it does, you may need time off or light duties for the first week. A medical certificate can be provided if required.

When can I start having sex again?

Generally you can resume sexual activity after a week — but you must consider yourself fertile until notified otherwise.

Do I need a GP referral?

A GP referral is not necessary.

How many days does it take to recover?

Recovery varies from a few days to a couple of weeks depending on the person. On average, men feel recovered after seven days.

Do you do the “laser” vasectomy?

Technically, “laser” vasectomy does not exist. Some vasectomists use a hyfrecator to cut the vas, which may confuse people — however, it is not “laser vasectomy”.

Can I use my private health insurance?

No — because we perform vasectomies in medical centres rather than private hospitals, private health insurance doesn't apply. The good news: it will almost certainly work out more affordable with us anyway.

Private health usually requires an excess of $500–$1,000, a urologist's gap fee, and an anaesthetist's fee with its own gap — often $2,000 or more out of pocket, with insurance. With us you know exactly what you'll pay: $597 out of pocket. No ifs, ands or maybes.
