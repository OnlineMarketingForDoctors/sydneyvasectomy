Before the procedure

The consultation comes first

Every man sees the doctor before anything else happens. It takes a few minutes and it exists to make sure this is genuinely the right decision for you.

  • Confirm your decision to have a vasectomy — a permanent sterilisation
  • Go through your medical history
  • Check you're not taking aspirin, warfarin or another blood thinner that would mean postponing
  • Review the consent form together, so the risks and possible complications are clear
  • A brief examination of the vas deferens

The procedure

Twelve steps, in the order they happen

Tap any step to see what it does. The diagram follows along.

01 · Getting ready The nurse is already waiting when you walk in.

  1. A nurse meets you in the procedure room. Trousers and underwear off, a sheet over you, and you wait on the bed while the last preparations are made.
  2. The scrotum is cleaned and prepared with betadine to reduce the chance of infection.
  3. A very fine needle places local anaesthetic into the skin of the scrotum. Most men do not notice the injection at all.
  4. A tiny opening is made in the front of the scrotum. One opening serves both sides — this is what makes it a no-scalpel vasectomy.
  5. More local anaesthetic is placed on each side, close to the vas deferens. This part causes a few seconds of discomfort, and then nothing.
  6. The left vas deferens is drawn out through the opening and the tissue lying over it is cleared away.
  7. A hyfrecator is used to divide the vas in half. (This is the instrument people sometimes mistake for a "laser vasectomy" — no such thing exists.)
  8. The end closest to the penis — the prostatic end — is blocked off, so sperm can no longer reach the ejaculate.
  9. A layer of tissue is placed between the prostatic and testicular ends. Called fascial interposition, it is what stops the two ends finding each other again.
  10. The testicular end is trimmed with scissors, deliberately left open, and returned to the scrotum. Leaving it open relieves pressure rather than building it up.
  11. The whole sequence is repeated on the right vas, through the same single opening.
  12. The opening is closed with steri-strips. That's it — you're free to go, with post-operative instructions, the doctor's contact details, and a pathology form for your semen analysis in three months.

The technique

There are two things worth understanding

Every vasectomy is described twice over: by how the surgeon reaches the vas, and by what they do with the testicular end afterwards. We do the gentler option on both counts.

Getting to the vas

Traditional

A scalpel makes an incision on each side of the scrotum to reach the vas — two wounds, more bruising, longer recovery.

No-scalpel what we do

One access point, opened by blunt dissection, reaching the vas on both sides. Less bruising and bleeding, and a quicker recovery.

What happens to the testicular end

Closed-ended

The testicular end is clamped shut with a clip or a suture. Pressure builds behind it.

Open-ended what we do

The end stays open, so sperm is released into the scrotum and reabsorbed. Think of a running tap with a kink in the hose — we take the kink out.

Before the day

Four things to sort out first

None of them take long. All of them matter.

Stop blood thinners

At least seven days before, stop any blood-thinning medication. Talk to your GP or specialist first, or call us on (02) 7911 3295.

Shave the morning of

Shave the scrotum with a razor on the morning of your procedure — we'll send you a picture showing exactly how much.

Plan the week after

If your job is physical, book time off or arrange light duties — no heavy lifting for seven days. We can write a medical certificate.

Sign the consent form

Three days before, we text you an electronic consent form. Read it, sign it on your phone, and there's nothing to fill in on the day.

Afterwards

The first three months

Some men bounce back in a couple of days, others take a fortnight. Seven days is the average.

  1. Day 0

    You drive yourself home. Take it easy, and use the post-operative instructions the doctor gives you.

  2. Days 1–3

    Mild pain and swelling, settling quickly. Bruising may appear — it fades over a week or so.

  3. Day 7

    Most men feel back to normal. No heavy lifting until now if your job is physical. Sex is generally fine again.

  4. Week 2

    If you're one of the slower healers, this is usually where it lands. Anything unexpected — call the doctor directly.

  5. About 3 months

    Semen analysis, using the pathology form you were given. Until we tell you it's clear, consider yourself fertile.

  6. All clear

    We confirm the result in writing. That's the point at which the vasectomy has worked.

Ask us anything

What is a vasectomy?

A simple procedure in which the vas deferens is cut, causing sterilisation. The vas is the tube that carries sperm from the testicles to the penis; on the way, sperm is joined by semen. Sperm makes up less than 5% of the ejaculate, so almost no one notices a change in volume afterwards.

How long does the procedure take?

Between 15 and 20 minutes.

Can I drive myself home?

Yes. It's done under local anaesthetic, so driving home afterwards is safe.

Can I be asleep for it?

We only offer local anaesthetic. If you'd prefer sedation or a general anaesthetic, ask your GP for a referral to a urologist.

Can a vasectomy be reversed?

Yes, but reversal is expensive, not covered by Medicare, and not guaranteed. If you're asking, you may want more time before committing to permanent contraception.

What are the risks?

Every surgical procedure carries some risk, and we do everything we can to keep complications rare. All of them are set out in your consent form.

Common and expected: bruising that fades over about a week; mild pain and swelling that settles within a few days.

Less common: scrotal haematoma — a large bruise inside the scrotum. The risk drops significantly if you follow the instructions about lifting in the days afterwards. Infection, which we guard against with strict protocols and which is usually mild and treated with oral antibiotics. And post-vasectomy pain syndrome, a rare complication with no agreed cause — pain often settles on its own, but in rare cases needs further surgery.

How do I know it worked?

A vasectomy does not mean immediate sterility. Consider yourself fertile until we tell you otherwise. A semen analysis about three months afterwards confirms all residual sperm has cleared.

When can I go back to work?

If your job doesn't involve heavy lifting, immediately. If it does, take some time off or ask for light duties for the first week. We can write a medical certificate.

When can I start having sex again?

Generally after a week — but you must consider yourself fertile until your semen analysis comes back clear.

Do I need a GP referral?

No.

How many days does recovery take?

Some men recover in a couple of days, others take up to two weeks. The average time to feeling back to normal is about seven days.

Do you do the “laser” vasectomy?

Technically, laser vasectomy doesn't exist. Some vasectomists use a hyfrecator to divide the vas, which is where the confusion comes from — but it isn't a laser.

Can I use my private health insurance?

No — we perform vasectomies in medical centres rather than private hospitals, and private health cover only helps when a urologist operates in a hospital or day surgery.

The good news is it almost certainly works out cheaper with us. Private health usually carries an excess of $500–$1,000, the urologist will have a gap on their fee, and nearly all surgeons want you asleep — which adds an anaesthetist's fee with its own gap. Add those together and $2,000 out of pocket is common. With insurance.

With us it's $597. No ifs, no ands, no "it depends on your health fund".
